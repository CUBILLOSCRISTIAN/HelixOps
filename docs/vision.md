# HelixOps - Vision

## Product Overview

HelixOps is a platform designed to monitor, manage, automate, and operate distributed systems from a centralized control plane.

The platform provides operational visibility, event processing, deployment automation, observability, and device management capabilities for organizations running distributed workloads across stores, branches, edge devices, and cloud environments.

HelixOps is designed following modern DevOps, Platform Engineering, and Observability principles, enabling teams to understand system health, automate operational workflows, and reduce manual intervention.

---

## Vision Statement

To provide a unified operational platform that enables organizations to observe, automate, and operate distributed systems reliably and efficiently.

---

## Mission

Build a platform that simplifies operational complexity by centralizing monitoring, event processing, deployment management, and infrastructure visibility into a single experience.

---

## Problem Statement

Organizations operating multiple locations, devices, services, and applications often struggle with:

- Limited visibility across distributed environments.
- Manual operational processes.
- Difficult deployment management.
- Fragmented monitoring solutions.
- Lack of centralized observability.
- Slow incident detection and response.

HelixOps aims to solve these challenges through a unified operational platform.

---

## Initial Target Scenario

The first implementation of HelixOps focuses on retail operations.

The platform will manage:

- Retail stores.
- POS devices.
- Distributed services.
- Sales event processing.
- Operational monitoring.
- Deployment visibility.

Retail serves as the initial domain to validate the platform architecture and operational workflows.

---

## Core Principles

### Observability First

Every operation must generate logs, metrics, and events.

### Automation First

Manual operational tasks should be minimized through automation.

### Event-Driven Design

Business and operational workflows should be modeled as events.

### Infrastructure as Code

Infrastructure should be reproducible and version-controlled.

### Cloud-Native Architecture

Services should be designed to run consistently across local and cloud environments.

### Operational Simplicity

The platform should remain understandable and operable by small teams.

---

## Core Capabilities

### Store Management

Manage distributed operational locations.

### Device Monitoring

Track health and status of connected devices.

### Event Processing

Process operational and business events asynchronously.

### Observability

Provide logs, metrics, traces, and operational insights.

### Deployment Management

Track deployments and operational changes.

### Alerting

Detect failures and notify operators.

---

## Non-Goals for MVP

The MVP will not include:

- Full ERP functionality.
- Accounting.
- Inventory optimization.
- Advanced analytics.
- Customer relationship management (CRM).
- Payment processing.
- Multi-cloud orchestration.

These capabilities may be evaluated in future iterations.

---

## Success Criteria

The MVP is considered successful when it can:

1. Register stores and devices.
2. Receive and process sales events.
3. Execute asynchronous workflows.
4. Generate operational telemetry.
5. Visualize system health.
6. Support automated deployments.
7. Run locally and in cloud environments using the same architecture.
